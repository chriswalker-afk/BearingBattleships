📐 Bearings maths help

These examples explain the skill without giving away where any creature is.

What is a bearing?

A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from north, written using three figures. For example, east is 090°, south is 180°, west is 270° and north is 000°.

How do I use the protractor?
  1. Drag its centre onto your ship.
  2. Rotate it so its 0° / N direction points upwards (north).
  3. Read clockwise from north to the creature, using its position on the circular scale.
  4. Type a three-figure bearing in Captain's orders.

Think of 045° as halfway between north and east, while 315° is northwest.

How do I use the ruler?

Move and rotate the ruler so it connects your ship to a creature. The board grid is spaced one nautical unit apart. Count squares or use the ruler's unit markings. Bubble range is up to 8 units; moving is limited to 1–5 units.

How do three-figure bearings work?

Always write three digits: 5° is 005°, 45° is 045°, and 120° remains 120°. 360° points north too, but this game uses 000°.

What if I measure an angle anticlockwise?

Bearings still go clockwise. If a direction is 40° anticlockwise from north, its bearing is 360° − 40° = 320°.

Worked example: firing northeast

A target is 3 squares east and 3 squares north of your ship. It is halfway between north and east: the bearing is 045°. Its distance is about 4.2 units, so a bubble with range 5 units reaches it. This is a generic example, not a game hint.

Why might a shot miss?

Check that your protractor is centred on the ship, 0° points north, you have measured clockwise, and your range is long enough. A bubble travels in a straight line along the bearing you enter.

Challenge: reverse bearings

The bearing from A to B is 070°. From B to A, turn 180°: the reverse bearing is 250°. If the original bearing is over 180°, subtract 180° instead.
